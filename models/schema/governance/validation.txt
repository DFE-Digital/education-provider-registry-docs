Why office_holder_assignment exists as a separate table

office_holder_assignment exists to solve a real modelling problem: a governance role
(e.g. Local governor, Academy trustee) and an office (Chair, Vice Chair) are independent
facts that change on different timelines and shouldn't be conflated into one field.

Why it's needed rather than just adding a role or is_chair flag on governance_appointment:

1. The underlying appointment must survive office changes. The logical model's own
   example makes this concrete: A.M. is a Local governor, gets elected Chair, later
   steps down as Chair but stays a Local governor. If "Chair" were just an attribute of
   the appointment row, you'd either overwrite history when the office ends, or you'd
   have to end and recreate the whole appointment just to change who chairs - which is
   wrong, since the underlying governorship never stopped.

2. Type inconsistency for URN: governance.governance_appointment.establishment_urn is
   integer, but establishment.establishment.urn (in the core establishment schema) is
   numeric. There's no FK between them (intentionally loose per the model), so it won't
   break anything today, but it's a latent trap if a future migration tries to join or
   FK across schemas.

3. Hardcoded role-ID allowlists in load/load-governance-appointment-fixture.sql
   (NOT IN (1,2,3,4,5,6,7), <> 8) duplicate what the governance_role_type seed table
   already encodes. If a new role type is ever added, both the seed data and this
   loader check need updating in lockstep - easy to miss the second one.

4. governance-schema.sql has a current_database() = 'governance_local' runtime guard;
   the sibling basic-establishment-data-schema.sql in the parent schema directory only
   has a comment, no equivalent guard. Not wrong, just an inconsistency in safety-net
   rigor between the two schema scripts if you want them to match.
