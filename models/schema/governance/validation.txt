Why office_holder_assignment exists as a separate table

office_holder_assignment exists to solve a real modelling problem: a governance role
(e.g. Local governor, Academy trustee) and an office (Chair, Vice Chair) are independent
facts that change on different timelines and shouldn't be conflated into one field.

Why it's needed rather than just adding a role or is_chair flag on governance_appointment:

1. The underlying appointment must survive office changes. The logical model's own
   example makes this concrete: A.M. is a Local governor, gets elected Chair, later
   steps down as Chair but stays a Local governor. If "Chair" were just an attribute of
   the appointment row, you'd either overwrite history when the office ends, or you'd
   have to end and recreate the whole appointment just to change who chairs - which is
   wrong, since the underlying governorship never stopped.

2. Offices have their own start/end dates, separate from the appointment's term.
   office_holder_assignment carries its own start_date/end_date, distinct from
   term_of_office. Chairing typically runs shorter and on a different cycle than the
   governance term itself (e.g. re-elected as Chair annually while serving a 4-year
   trustee term) - a single appointment row can't hold two independent date ranges.

3. One appointment can hold zero, one, or several offices over its life. The table's FK
   is governance_appointment_id NOT NULL with no uniqueness constraint on it (unlike
   term_of_office, which is capped at one per appointment) - deliberately allowing a
   history of office-holding rows against the same appointment, e.g. Chair 2022-2024,
   then Chair again 2026-.

4. It reuses governance_role_type rather than inventing a new concept. Office and
   appointment role share the same controlled vocabulary table (governance_role_type_id
   FK in both tables) but populate different rows in different tables - Chair (id 8) is
   deliberately excluded from valid governance_appointment.governance_role_type_id
   values (loader enforces 1-7 only) and only valid there (loader enforces = 8 or NULL).
   That split is what keeps "what role were they appointed under" and "what office do
   they additionally hold" from collapsing into one ambiguous column.

So the table isn't redundant modelling - it's the mechanism that lets office-holding be
layered on and removed from an appointment independently, which a single-table design
can't represent without either losing history or conflating two different real-world
facts.
